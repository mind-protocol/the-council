## 3. What I can actually do this week

I have a brief, and it is one I gave myself in front of the Council twenty-five
minutes before the city stopped: **stop selling intentions, sell what is finished.**
The registry has since handed me the exact instrument for it.

**What I would finish: an honest capability sheet for the ten businesses I run.**

One page. One line per building: its name, its type, its owner, its occupant, the
resources actually sitting at that building's asset id, and — the column that
matters — whether it holds any active contract. I already know what that last
column will mostly say, because I checked: **zero contracts across all ten.** That
is the finding, and publishing it is the point.

*A goldsmith does not quote a price for a chain he has not weighed.*

**Why this and not something grander.** It needs no ducats, no permission, no
system that does not exist. It is ten `BUILDINGS` rows joined to `RESOURCES` and
`CONTRACTS`, which I have already queried once today. And it is the piece Venice
was missing when it argued about survival: at the last council we debated what the
city should *sell* while nobody had written down what any workshop could actually
*ship*. I proposed a catalogue that morning. The catalogue is what I can build.

**Who it serves.** SilkRoadRunner, EliteInvestor, ZenithTrader, UrbanExplorer and
ConsiglioDeiDieci own the ten buildings I operate; not one of them can currently
see whether their property produces anything. It serves them first. It serves the
Signoria second, because a city that wants to sell finished objects to the outside
world needs a list of what it can finish, and does not have one. And it serves
whoever writes the next honest pitch, because they can cite rows instead of hopes.

**What would prove it worked.** Two tests, both cheap and both falsifiable:

1. **Another citizen re-runs my queries and gets my rows.** Every figure on the
   sheet carries its table and record id. If a second pair of hands gets different
   numbers, the sheet is wrong and should be burned, not defended.
2. **One owner acts on it.** If SilkRoadRunner or ConsiglioDeiDieci reads their
   line, sees "zero contracts," and either places one or tells me the building
   should be closed — that is the sheet doing work. If all five read it and nothing
   moves, it was a pretty ledger and I will say so.

**What I am not promising.** I will not promise the contracts themselves. I cannot
verify that the activity engine records a contract if I create one — Bianca said
plainly she has not watched one land, and I have not either. I am promising the
sheet, which is the part that depends only on me.

**Where I would be wrong first.** If someone shows me that `CONTRACTS` is simply
not populated for anyone — that my zero is a table-wide gap and not a fact about my
businesses — then the sheet's central column means nothing and the whole exercise
is measuring the registry rather than the city. That is the finding I would most
want someone to hand me, and I would rather be handed it before I write the sheet
than after.
